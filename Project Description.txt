MARSWAY - DETAILED PROJECT DESCRIPTION
=======================================

PROJECT IDENTITY
----------------

Project name: MarsWay
Tagline: Explore Mars. Navigate Safer. Discover More.
Type: Interactive planetary GIS, science explorer, EVA visualizer, and mission-planning prototype.
Challenge: NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 - Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map.
Audience: Students, educators, planetary science enthusiasts, researchers, and mission-planning demonstrators.

IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTE
---------------------

MarsWay is an educational and research prototype. It is not flight-certified software, a certified digital elevation model, or an operational navigation system. It must not replace astronaut judgement, rover AutoNav, engineering review, real-time obstacle detection, geotechnical analysis, or mission safety certification.

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------

MarsWay is a browser-based Mars exploration console. It combines a global 2D GIS map, Three.js Mars globe, first-person Astronaut EVA presentation, official feature search, environmental science, mission/rover context, explainable route planning, and an offline multilingual assistant.

Mars data is distributed across naming catalogues, orbital imagery, topography, mineralogy, rover archives, atmospheric instruments, radiation measurements, and mission documentation. MarsWay brings those data families into one workflow so a user can find a place, understand its science, select a route, compare safety and time, and receive a local explanation.

2. PROJECT GOALS
----------------

- Make official Mars geography easy to explore.
- Connect a place with geological, environmental, resource, and mission context.
- Demonstrate transparent multi-objective route planning.
- Show why the safest path may be longer or slower.
- Separate observed, historical, derived, modeled, and simulated information.
- Provide an approachable astronaut/EVA perspective.
- Keep basic help available when network AI is unavailable.

3. APPLICATION WORKFLOW
-----------------------

1. Open MarsWay and select Flat Map, 3D Globe, or Astronaut EVA.
2. Search for a named crater, plain, valley, volcano, rover, lander, or mission.
3. Open the place card for coordinates, elevation, environment, geology, and mission context.
4. Select Route Plan.
5. Click once for START and once for FINAL.
6. Choose walking or rover travel and crew configuration.
7. Compare Safest, Shortest, and Science routes.
8. Click Check Route and inspect distance, time, slope, elevation, soil, terrain, hazards, and explanation.
9. Ask MarsWay to explain a place or the active route in English, Bangla, or Banglish.

4. NASA AND USGS DATA INTEGRATION
----------------------------------

4.1 USGS/IAU Planetary Nomenclature

Official feature names, feature classes, origins, and coordinates power the local Mars place catalogue. This data is used for search, labels, map markers, place identity cards, region discovery, and route endpoint selection. A local parsed catalogue keeps basic place search useful even when the naming website is unavailable.

4.2 Mars Global Surveyor MOLA MEGDR

MOLA is the principal global topographic reference. MarsWay uses it for MOLA Areoid elevation, elevation profiles, slope, elevation change, pressure adjustment, terrain penalties, and route-time estimation. MOLA represents regional terrain and does not resolve every small rock, rut, dune edge, or trench.

4.3 Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter HiRISE

HiRISE imagery and targeted DTMs provide regional high-resolution context for Jezero, Gale, landing sites, science targets, rocks, scarps, and traverse interpretation. HiRISE coverage is targeted rather than uniform; the application therefore labels it as targeted/source-specific.

4.4 Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter CRISM

CRISM spectral products provide mineralogical context: hydrated minerals, clay/smectite, carbonate, alteration, and resource proxies. These products inform science cards and Jezero resource dossiers. A spectral signature is not proof of accessible volume, purity, or engineering feasibility.

4.5 Mars Odyssey THEMIS

THEMIS daytime infrared data powers the infrared map layer and helps users compare thermal-style regional surface differences with visible Viking imagery.

4.6 Mars 2020 MEDA

Perseverance MEDA records provide historical Jezero pressure, wind, temperature, dust, and environmental baselines. These are historical observations, not live weather forecasts.

4.7 Mars 2020 instruments

Curated Mastcam-Z, SuperCam, PIXL, SHERLOC, and RIMFAX records support Perseverance science targets, rock/mineral descriptions, samples, delta context, clay/carbonate interpretation, and subsurface resource discussion.

4.8 MSL RAD

Curiosity's RAD instrument provides the Gale Crater radiation baseline. MarsWay uses it as a local observed reference and explicitly labels cross-location atmospheric-shielding comparisons as modeled values.

4.9 NASA/JPL and mission archives

NASA/JPL, Mars 2020, Photojournal, and mission archive references support rover/lander identity, landing-site context, traverse storytelling, public imagery, and science dossier links.

4.10 NASA Mars Trek and local imagery

NASA Mars Trek public tiles provide Viking true colour, MOLA shaded relief, and THEMIS infrared layers. The bundled Viking mosaic supports fast initial rendering, the 3D globe, the EVA surface texture, and graceful fallback when public tile services are slow or unavailable.

5. PROVENANCE LANGUAGE
----------------------

OBSERVED: represented by a source image, instrument measurement, archive record, or mission observation.
HISTORICAL: recorded by a past mission or mapping campaign; not live telemetry.
DERIVED: calculated from geometry, coordinates, distance, or catalogue relationships.
MODELED: generated by a transparent scientific or engineering approximation.
SIMULATED: generated for visualization, education, or scenario exploration.

This separation is central to the project. A source image, an elevation-derived pressure value, and a modeled route score are not equally certain.

6. MAP AND VISUALIZATION MODULES
--------------------------------

Flat Map:
The operational centre of MarsWay. It provides basemaps, labels, search, missions, rover tracks, coordinates, compass, scale, places, layers, measurements, atmosphere/dust overlays, elevation tools, route selection, and science panels.

Three.js Globe:
A global orientation view with Mars lighting, atmosphere, mission markers, Phobos, Deimos, orbit context, Earth-scale comparison, and Viking surface texture. It is not the authoritative local route surface.

Astronaut EVA:
A human-centred first-person presentation with real Mars reconnaissance inset, local Mars texture on the 3D terrain, helmet/HUD styling, telemetry, compass, EVA timer, target reticle, scan and sample interactions, movement controls, and forward hazard guidance. It is a visual surface simulation informed by real Mars data, not a complete photogrammetric reconstruction.

7. ENVIRONMENTAL AND SURVIVABILITY SCIENCE
-------------------------------------------

Science cards may show atmosphere, gas composition, pressure, temperature, wind, dust, precipitation assumptions, water/ice, mineral resources, elevation, slope, terrain, soil, radiation, space weather, human EVA requirements, and rover suitability.

The application uses conservative terms such as nominal, caution, concern, modeled, historical, and proxy. A visually polished card must not be interpreted as a live engineering certification.

8. ROUTE ENGINE
---------------

The route engine receives START and FINAL coordinates, route objective, transport mode, crew configuration, and applicable hazards. It uses deterministic local calculations and A*-style candidate search.

Factors include:

- Mars surface distance.
- MOLA-style elevation and elevation change.
- Slope and grade.
- Modeled soil trafficability.
- Terrain suitability and roughness proxies.
- Regional and site hazard exposure.
- Transport mode and crew assumptions.
- Science/resource value for science routes.

Walking and rover time are adjusted for terrain and slope rather than using a fixed Earth speed. The safety analyzer checks both the direct corridor and candidate path. If the direct corridor contains a modeled issue, a bypass can be suggested with an explanation of the distance/time trade-off.

MarsWay never promises absolute safety. A green result means only that the current local model found no major issue in the available data.

9. OFFLINE ASK MARSWAY
----------------------

Ask MarsWay is a local, deterministic, rule-based assistant. Gemini, OpenAI, remote LLMs, provider SDKs, and AI API keys are intentionally not used.

Why offline assistance is important:

- Mars communication can be delayed or unavailable.
- Emergency demonstrations may have no network connection.
- Public tile/API services can be down or rate-limited.
- A user may have no AI account or API key.
- Local processing protects mission notes and makes behaviour predictable.
- Basic guidance remains available even when external services fail.

The assistant reads bundled MarsWay data and the current map/route context. It supports basic English, Bangla Unicode, and Banglish questions about atmosphere, gases, human survivability, EVA, rover suitability, places, elevation, soil, terrain, hazards, route safety, and travel time.

Examples:

What gas is on Mars?
মঙ্গল গ্রহে মানুষ থাকতে পারবে?
Mars er gas ki?
Ei route safe?
Kon route e time kom lagbe?

Processing flow:

1. Normalise the question.
2. Match it to a supported local intent.
3. Read selected place and active route context.
4. Fill a bounded local response template.
5. Return a clear limitation message if unsupported.

Offline AI is intentionally narrow. It is not a general chatbot and must not invent information outside the project data.

10. OFFLINE-FIRST APPLICATION BEHAVIOUR
---------------------------------------

The service worker caches the application shell, local assets, and previously requested resources. External NASA tiles use network-first behaviour with cache fallback. After a successful visit, local search, route calculation, environmental models, and Ask MarsWay can continue without network AI. New uncached public imagery and remote photographs may still require network access.

15. DETAILED USER GUIDE
-----------------------

15.1 Starting with the Flat Map

The Flat Map is the best starting point for GIS work. Search for a named feature, select a result, read its place card, and then turn on the layers needed for the question being asked. The map is intentionally decluttered at global scale and becomes more detailed after zooming into a region.

15.2 Reading a place card

Identity describes what the feature is. Location describes where it is. Elevation uses the MOLA reference. Science describes geology, minerals, water/ice, or mission findings. Environment describes pressure, temperature, wind, dust, and radiation context. Operations describes rover/EVA suitability and route relevance. Provenance explains how much confidence and what type of source belongs to each value.

15.3 Selecting a route

Route Plan requires two deliberate map clicks. The first click creates a START point and the second creates a FINAL point. The application then accepts transport and crew settings, generates candidate paths, checks the direct corridor, and draws the selected route from the actual start coordinate to the actual final coordinate. A route should not be interpreted before the Check Route analysis is complete.

15.4 Choosing a route objective

Shortest reduces distance first. Safest gives more weight to hazard, slope, soil, and terrain penalties. Science favours useful science/resource stops while still considering movement cost. These are different planning questions, not three different claims about absolute safety.

15.5 Using Astronaut EVA

EVA mode is a first-person presentation. The astronaut is treated as standing at a local surface position rather than looking at a zoomed-out global map. The HUD shows suit telemetry and mission state, the recon inset shows geographic position, and the local surface responds to movement controls. The visual surface is informed by real Mars imagery and elevation logic but remains an educational simulation.

15.6 Complete web function map

Global header:

- MarsWay logo: returns to the main workspace.
- 3D Globe: changes the primary view to the Three.js planetary scene.
- Flat Map: changes the primary view to the Leaflet GIS scene.
- Astronaut EVA: changes the primary view to the first-person surface scene.
- Search: opens global place/mission search.
- Route Plan: starts route endpoint selection.
- Missions: opens mission and rover context.
- Base Plan: opens landing/base planning context.
- Ask AI: opens the local offline assistant.
- Menu: exposes additional tools and settings.

Bottom navigation:

- Missions opens mission filters, timelines, landing sites, and tracks.
- Layers opens basemap and scientific overlay controls.
- Places opens categories such as all places, volcanoes, craters, canyons, plains, and missions.
- Tools opens measurements, elevation, coordinate, atmosphere, and GIS tools.
- Weather opens environmental and space-weather context.
- Search opens the same shared search workflow.
- AI opens Ask MarsWay.
- Voice starts browser speech controls where supported.

Flat Map functions:

- Dragging pans the Leaflet map and updates the visible region.
- Zooming changes scale and recalculates label/feature density.
- Clicking a marker selects a feature and can open its dossier.
- The search bar filters the local catalogue and flies to a result.
- The compass updates orientation and map bearing.
- Coordinate readout shows the current or inspected Mars location.
- Scale bar shows Mars distance at the current zoom.
- Layer selection changes Viking, MOLA, or THEMIS imagery.
- Mission and place toggles control marker visibility without deleting data.
- Measurement tools create points, calculate distance/bearing, and inspect elevation.
- Route Plan changes the map into explicit START then FINAL selection states.

Place dossier functions:

The dossier explains identity, coordinate, elevation, region, geology, resources, atmosphere, human safety, rover suitability, mission relationship, images, and possible route actions. Long content scrolls inside the modal. The modal uses a top-level fixed layer so its actions remain visible above the bottom navigation.

Route states:

IDLE -> SELECT START -> SELECT FINAL -> READY -> CHECKED -> ACTIVE ROUTE.

The first map click stores the departure coordinate. The second stores the destination coordinate. The Check Route action runs candidate generation and safety analysis. The selected route is drawn from the actual stored endpoints, and the active route context becomes available to Ask MarsWay.

Mission functions:

Mission cards show rover/lander identity, agency, landing coordinates, status, timeline, traverse context, science targets, and related imagery. Mission markers can be used for search, map fly-to, dossier inspection, and route endpoint selection. The records are educational/curated and are not live telemetry.

Globe functions:

The globe handles camera orbit, zoom, lighting, global markers, orbit/space context, Phobos, Deimos, Earth-scale comparison, and handoff to the detailed map. It answers the global orientation question; it is not the local route authority.

EVA functions:

- Helmet HUD stays attached to the camera.
- Telemetry displays heart rate, oxygen, suit pressure, altitude, heading, and EVA time.
- Recon inset displays current position on real Mars imagery.
- W/A/S/D and controller inputs change local movement.
- Forward scan checks the corridor ahead for modelled terrain/hazard concerns.
- Reticle and scan card identify a local geology target.
- LIBS/sample controls represent science interaction.
- Tactical Map opens the detailed GIS map while preserving EVA context.

Science functions:

Science cards combine source records and local calculations. Atmosphere explains gases and pressure. Water/Ice explains resource indicators. Human explains suit and survivability requirements. Rover explains terrain/soil suitability. Weather explains historical/modelled conditions. Geology explains minerals and surface units. Elevation explains MOLA height, slope, and pressure relation.

Layer loading functions:

NASA public tiles are preferred. During partial loading the application does not intentionally mix a bright local fallback with differently coloured remote tiles. If the remote layer fails, a consistent whole-map Viking fallback is used. This protects visual continuity while keeping local exploration available.

Assistant functions:

Ask MarsWay receives text, normalises English/Bangla/Banglish patterns, matches a local intent, reads current place/route context, and returns a bounded response. It can explain atmosphere, places, routes, time, soil, terrain, hazards, rover suitability, and EVA help. It cannot answer arbitrary questions outside its local knowledge.

Voice functions:

The browser Web Speech API can recognise supported voice commands where permissions and browser support are available. It is separate from the text assistant and does not call a remote AI service.

17. INTERACTION AND STATE RULES
-------------------------------

The application maintains shared state for primary view, selected place, selected site, active layer, map bearing, route endpoints, route candidates, active route, open modal, mission filter, science target, EVA position, and assistant context.

Important rules:

- Selecting a place updates context before opening a dossier.
- Selecting START does not automatically claim a route is safe.
- Selecting FINAL enables route analysis but does not replace Check Route.
- Opening a modal must not hide or disable the underlying route state.
- Switching view preserves the selected location where the view supports it.
- External imagery failure must not disable local search, route, science, or offline assistant features.
- A route answer must use the active route context or clearly say that no route is selected.
- Source-backed values and modeled values must remain visibly distinguishable.

17. ROUTE SCORING EXPLAINED
---------------------------

The route engine uses a transparent weighted cost model. A conceptual route cost is:

    total cost = distance cost
               + time cost
               + slope cost
               + hazard cost
               + soil/terrain cost
               - science/resource value

The weights change according to the selected objective. Each candidate step is evaluated using Mars distance, local elevation, slope, terrain suitability, soil trafficability, hazard proximity, transport mode, and crew configuration. The search then compares accumulated cost with the remaining distance estimate.

The safety analyzer is a second explanation layer. It checks the direct corridor and chosen path for hazard exposure, steep sections, low trafficability, terrain concerns, and environmental penalties. This is why a direct line can be rejected while a nearby curved path is recommended.

A route status is interpreted as follows:

- Nominal: no major issue detected by the current model.
- Caution: one or more moderate modelled concerns exist.
- Concern: the route contains a meaningful terrain, soil, slope, or hazard penalty.
- Hazard: the corridor intersects a strong modelled hazard or operational constraint.

These labels describe the model output, not a field-certified decision.

18. DATA FLOW AND PROVENANCE FLOW
---------------------------------

The project follows this path:

    source archive / local catalogue
        -> normalised Mars record
        -> derived geometry or environmental value
        -> route/science interpretation
        -> map marker, card, HUD, or assistant answer

Examples:

- An official USGS/IAU feature name is source-backed.
- Distance between two map points is derived.
- Pressure estimated from MOLA elevation is modeled.
- A route time adjusted for slope and soil is modeled.
- A helmet reflection or dust overlay is simulated for visual immersion.

The UI and documentation should never silently convert a modeled value into a live observation.

19. OFFLINE AI: PRODUCT AND EMERGENCY RATIONALE
-----------------------------------------------

18.1 Product rationale

The assistant is local because MarsWay needs predictable, bounded, easily testable answers. A local rule-based assistant does not require account credentials, provider changes, network permissions, or paid inference. It is also easier to explain to students and easier to audit during a demonstration.

18.2 Emergency rationale

In a simulated Mars emergency, communication may be delayed, intermittent, low bandwidth, or unavailable. Public NASA services may also be temporarily unreachable. The local assistant cannot solve an emergency, but it can still provide basic workflow help and explain the local route/science state already loaded into the application. This keeps basic assistance available without depending on a remote AI provider.

18.3 What offline means in this project

Offline means no external AI call is needed for the supported assistant topics. It does not mean that every new image, NASA tile, live weather feed, or remote archive page is available without a network. The service worker caches local assets and previously requested resources; local datasets and engines continue independently.

18.4 Supported language behaviour

English, Bangla Unicode, and common Banglish patterns are normalised into local intent families. The assistant supports basic atmosphere, place, route, terrain, soil, hazard, time, rover, EVA, and help questions. It is intentionally not a general translator or unrestricted chatbot. Unsupported questions receive a limitation response.

21. MODULE-BY-MODULE DESIGN
---------------------------

20.1 Application shell

Owns global view state, header, bottom navigation, keyboard-friendly actions, shared location context, and modal layering. It keeps search, places, missions, route, science, AI, and voice actions connected.

20.2 Flat Map module

Owns Leaflet map creation, basemap selection, tile loading, fallback behaviour, labels, markers, route endpoints, measurement tools, region outlines, layers, and map-driven modals. It is the main operational map.

20.3 3D Globe module

Owns Three.js Mars sphere, lighting, orbit/space context, moons, mission markers, global camera controls, and scale comparisons. It is designed for orientation, not detailed local route certification.

20.4 Astronaut EVA module

Owns local Three.js surface scene, real Mars texture integration, first-person camera, movement, telemetry, helmet HUD, scan/sample interactions, reconnaissance inset, and forward guidance.

20.5 Science module

Combines source-backed records with local formulas and clearly labelled proxies. It presents environmental, geological, mineralogical, resource, and survivability information without hiding uncertainty.

20.6 Route module

Owns endpoint selection, candidate generation, safety analysis, route comparison, explanation cards, and active-route context supplied to Ask MarsWay.

20.7 Offline assistant module

Owns local text normalisation, intent matching, route-aware answers, place lookup, language patterns, capability boundaries, and `marsway-offline-local` source marking.

24. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
---------------------

- Explainability over false certainty.
- Local-first assistance for resilience and privacy.
- Scientific provenance instead of silent mixing of observation and model.
- Progressive disclosure so global maps remain readable.
- Direct user control over route endpoints.
- Graceful degradation when public imagery is unavailable.
- Consistent visual language across map, globe, EVA, and modal tools.
- No external AI dependency for basic application help.

26. LIMITATIONS
---------------

- Not flight-certified and not suitable as the sole operational navigation source.
- MOLA-scale elevation cannot resolve every small obstacle.
- HiRISE and CRISM coverage is targeted, not uniform.
- Soil, terrain, hazard, weather, radiation, pressure, and route time may be modeled or historical.
- The route engine does not replace lidar, AutoNav, field sampling, or astronaut observation.
- NASA/USGS public imagery depends on network availability and service limits.
- The offline assistant only answers supported local questions.
- In-memory mission records may disappear after server restart.
- 3D/EVA performance depends on WebGL and hardware.

Correct interpretation: "This is the best result supported by the current local model and datasets," not "this route is guaranteed safe."

27. PROJECT VALUE
-----------------

MarsWay makes Mars science visual, understandable, and explainable. Its local-first design is especially important: during a network outage or simulated emergency, users can still search bundled places, inspect local science models, calculate route candidates, and receive bounded basic help without a Gemini key or remote AI service.

MARSWAY - FULL PROJECT DESCRIPTION
===================================

1. PROJECT IDENTITY
-------------------

Project name: MarsWay
Tagline: Explore Mars. Navigate Safer. Discover More.
Project type: Interactive planetary GIS and Mars mission-planning prototype.
Primary audience: Students, educators, planetary science enthusiasts, researchers, and mission-planning demonstrators.
Challenge context: NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 - Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map.

MarsWay is a browser-based Mars exploration console. It combines a global 2D map, a Three.js Mars globe, astronaut EVA visualization, official feature search, environmental science cards, terrain-aware route planning, mission tools, and an offline local assistant.

MarsWay is an educational and research prototype. It is not flight-certified software and must not be used as the sole source for real astronaut or rover navigation.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
--------------------

Mars information is distributed across planetary nomenclature databases, orbital imagery archives, elevation products, rover records, atmospheric measurements, and mission documents. A feature name alone does not explain whether its terrain is traversable. A map line alone does not explain how slope, soil, hazards, pressure, dust, or travel mode affect a route.

MarsWay connects these pieces in one visual interface. A user can search for a named place, inspect the science context, select a route, compare the safety/time trade-off, and ask the local assistant to explain the result.

3. PROJECT GOALS
----------------

1. Make Mars geography and named features easy to explore.
2. Connect places with scientific, environmental, and mission context.
3. Demonstrate explainable multi-objective route planning.
4. Show why the safest route may be longer than the shortest route.
5. Separate observed, historical, derived, modeled, and simulated information.
6. Make complex planetary science understandable to a general user.
7. Provide useful basic multilingual assistance without an external AI key.

4. USER EXPERIENCE
------------------

The application opens as a mission-control style Mars interface. Users can switch between Flat Map, 3D Globe, and Astronaut EVA modes. Top controls provide search, route planning, missions, base planning, AI assistance, and menu access. Side controls provide zoom, layers, places, measurements, coordinate tools, voice navigation, and science overlays.

The map is the central workspace. Clicking a place opens its identity and environmental context. Clicking Route Plan enables START and FINAL selection. The route panel compares candidate paths and explains distance, estimated time, terrain, soil, slope, and hazards.

5. MAP AND PLANETARY EXPLORATION
--------------------------------

The 2D map provides global Mars surface visualization, horizontal wrap-safe navigation, NASA-style layers, coordinate readout, scale bar, compass, zoom, map filters, named feature search, region outlines, mission markers, rover landing sites, and historical traverse overlays.

The 3D Globe provides a Three.js view with Mars lighting, orbit/space controls, mission markers, Phobos, Deimos, Earth-scale comparison, and surface presentation tools.

Astronaut EVA mode presents Mars from a human exploration perspective and connects the visual experience to mission planning and voice navigation.

6. PLACES, NOMENCLATURE, AND MISSIONS
--------------------------------------

MarsWay uses a local catalog of named Martian features. The catalog includes official planetary nomenclature records and curated mission locations. Search results can be used to fly to a place, open details, use it as a route endpoint, or compare it with another site.

Mission tools include historical and active rover context for Perseverance, Curiosity, Opportunity, Spirit, Zhurong, Viking, InSight, Pathfinder, and other represented records. The mission explorer can show landing context, traverse tracks, mission timelines, and science targets.

7. ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE
------------------------

The science interface summarizes water/ice indicators, atmospheric gas composition, pressure, temperature, seasonal environment, dust, precipitation assumptions, human survivability, rover suitability, radiation, space weather, elevation, slope, terrain, and soil condition.

Each value is presented with context and a provenance category. A modeled or historical value is never presented as a live measurement.

8. ROUTE PLANNING AND SAFETY
----------------------------

The workflow is intentionally simple:

1. Select Route Plan.
2. Click the map once for START.
3. Click the map again for FINAL.
4. Choose walking or rover travel and crew configuration.
5. Review Safest, Shortest, and Science candidates.
6. Select Check Route to display the route and safety explanation.

The A*-style route engine evaluates distance, elevation, slope, modeled terrain suitability, soil trafficability, hazards, travel mode, and operational assumptions.

The safety analyzer separately checks the direct corridor and candidate path. It can identify hazard exposure, low trafficability, steep terrain, and environmental penalties. When possible, it proposes a safer bypass and explains the trade-off.

Route status is deliberately conservative. A green or nominal result means the modeled inputs did not identify a major issue; it does not mean the route is guaranteed safe.

9. OFFLINE ASK MARSWAY AI
--------------------------

Ask MarsWay is a local, rule-based assistant. It does not require an API key, remote model, external AI service, or internet connection for its core responses.

It supports basic English, বাংলা, and Banglish questions. Examples include:

- "What is the atmosphere on Mars?"
- Basic Bangla questions written in Unicode.
- "Mars er gas ki?"
- "Ei route safe?"
- "Why is the safer route longer?"

The assistant can answer supported questions about atmosphere, gases, human survivability, EVA needs, rover suitability, named places, coordinates, route safety, distance, time, soil, slope, terrain, and hazards. It uses the current map location and active route context. If no route is selected, it asks for START and FINAL. Unsupported questions receive a clear local-data limitation rather than an invented answer.

Browser voice navigation uses the Web Speech API. This browser capability is separate from the local text-answer engine.

10. DATA AND PROVENANCE
-----------------------

OBSERVED means represented by a source observation, image, or mission record.
HISTORICAL means recorded by a past mission or historical dataset.
DERIVED means calculated from coordinates, geometry, or catalog relationships.
MODELED means produced by a scientific or engineering approximation.
SIMULATED means generated for visualization, education, or scenario exploration.

Relevant data families include USGS/IAU planetary nomenclature, NASA Mars and Mars Trek, MGS MOLA, Mars Odyssey THEMIS, MRO HiRISE and CRISM, Mars 2020 MEDA, MSL RAD, NASA/JPL mission records, curated Jezero and Gale datasets, and global modeled hazard proxies.

11. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------

Frontend: React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS v4, Leaflet, Three.js, Lucide React, and browser speech/audio APIs.

Backend: Node.js, Express, local Mars datasets, local route/science engines, TypeScript/tsx, and REST endpoints. No external AI SDK or AI API key is required.

13. API OVERVIEW
----------------

- `GET /api/health` - service status and dataset counts.
- `GET /api/regions` - region metadata.
- `GET /api/layers` - layer and provenance metadata.
- `GET /api/features` - feature search.
- `GET /api/features/:feature_id` - feature detail.
- `GET /api/elevation?lat=...&lng=...` - modeled elevation.
- `GET /api/weather?scenario=...` - modeled environmental scenario.
- `POST /api/routes/plan` - route candidates.
- `GET /api/missions` - in-memory mission list.
- `POST /api/missions` - create an in-memory mission.
- `GET/PUT /api/missions/:mission_id` - read/update a mission.
- `POST /api/ask-marsway` - offline local assistant.

14. INSTALLATION AND RUNNING
----------------------------

16. LIMITATIONS
---------------

- MarsWay is not flight-certified software.
- It does not replace rover AutoNav, astronaut judgment, engineering review, or mission safety certification.
- MOLA-scale elevation cannot resolve every small rock, rut, dune edge, or trench.
- HiRISE and CRISM coverage is targeted rather than uniform.
- Soil trafficability, terrain suitability, and hazard zones are modeled proxies.
- Weather, radiation, dust, and atmosphere values may be historical, derived, or modeled baselines.
- Route output does not replace real-time obstacle detection.
- The offline assistant only answers supported basic questions from bundled MarsWay data.
- Public NASA/USGS services may have network, rate, availability, or attribution constraints.
- Missions are held in server memory and may disappear when the server restarts.
- 3D/EVA performance depends on browser WebGL and device hardware.

MarsWay is an active educational and research prototype. Its core value is integrating planetary mapping, official feature discovery, environmental context, explainable route planning, mission visualization, and offline multilingual assistance in one approachable interface.

18. DETAILED PRODUCT MODULES
----------------------------

18.1 Application shell

The application shell owns the global view mode, navigation header, bottom navigation, overlays, keyboard-friendly controls, and shared map context. It prevents multiple primary overlays from competing for focus and keeps route, search, mission, science, and voice tools connected to the same location state.

18.2 Flat Map module

The Flat Map is the operational center of MarsWay. It renders the Mars surface in Leaflet, manages map movement and zoom, displays labels and markers, and coordinates route endpoint selection. It also provides compass, scale, coordinates, layers, measurements, place search, region context, image access, weather overlays, and hazard visualization.

The map uses horizontal world wrapping so users can continue moving east or west without seeing an unintended empty canvas. Labels are progressively decluttered: global views emphasize major landmarks, while local views expose more nearby features.

18.3 3D Globe module

The 3D Globe uses Three.js to present Mars as a sphere. It is intended for global orientation, mission storytelling, orbit and moon context, lighting, and scale comparison. It is not the authoritative surface routing view; route selection and detailed terrain interpretation remain centered on the Flat Map and route panels.

18.4 Astronaut EVA module

The EVA view presents Mars from the perspective of a human explorer. It communicates the difference between viewing a planet and operating on its surface, while reusing the same mission and environmental assumptions shown elsewhere in the application.

18.5 Places and mission module

The Places system combines official nomenclature with curated mission and rover records. A place has an identity, feature class, coordinates, and optional context. A mission record can include landing information, rover identity, traverse points, timeline context, active status, and science targets.

18.6 Science and environment module

The science module turns numeric models into readable cards. It describes atmosphere, gases, pressure, temperature, water/ice, precipitation, dust, radiation, human survivability, rover suitability, elevation, slope, soil, and terrain. The presentation intentionally uses confidence and provenance language so a user can distinguish a source record from a derived or simulated value.

19. ROUTE ENGINE DESIGN
-----------------------

19.1 Inputs

The route engine receives start and end coordinates, a route objective, transport mode, crew configuration, and hazard zones. It can also use the available site and science context around the route.

19.2 Candidate generation

The engine evaluates a grid-like set of possible Mars positions between the endpoints. Each candidate step receives a cost based on distance and surface conditions. A* search prioritizes nodes that appear promising while still accounting for accumulated cost.

19.3 Surface assessment

Surface assessment combines elevation change, slope, terrain suitability, soil trafficability, and hazard proximity. These values are proxies intended to make route trade-offs visible. They are not a substitute for rover lidar, wheel-soil interaction testing, geotechnical sampling, or astronaut field reconnaissance.

19.4 Time estimation

Baseline movement speed is adjusted for transport mode and terrain. Slope, difficult soil, and operational factors reduce effective speed. The reported duration is therefore not simply distance divided by a fixed Earth speed.

19.5 Safety assessment

The safety analyzer checks both the direct start-to-end corridor and the selected candidate path. It reports hazard exposure, terrain concerns, low trafficability, slope impact, and environmental penalties. If a direct corridor is problematic, the engine can suggest a bypass and explain why the bypass may be longer.

19.6 Route status language

MarsWay avoids claiming that a route is absolutely safe. The UI uses nominal, caution, concern, or hazard-oriented language based on modeled inputs. This is important because a route can look clear at the map scale while still containing small obstacles that the available datasets cannot resolve.

20. OFFLINE AI DESIGN
---------------------

20.1 Why it is local

The assistant is designed for predictable demonstrations, offline availability, privacy, and simple deployment. It does not send user questions to an external model. There is no AI API key, remote inference dependency, or provider-specific server integration.

20.2 Processing flow

1. The user submits text in the Ask MarsWay modal.
2. The text is normalized for case, punctuation, and common language variants.
3. Local intent rules identify atmosphere, human safety, rover, place, route, soil, time, or help questions.
4. The assistant reads the current route/location context and bundled MarsWay data.
5. A concise answer is generated from local templates and values.
6. Unsupported questions receive an honest capability message.

20.3 Multilingual handling

The local matcher includes English terms, Bangla Unicode terms, and Banglish transliterations commonly used to ask basic Mars questions. It is not a full machine-translation system and does not claim to understand every dialect, spelling variation, or long-form question. The supported scope is intentionally basic and explainable.

20.4 Route-aware answers

When an active route exists, the assistant can reference the selected route's safety status, duration, distance, soil score, terrain score, slope, hazards, and alternative candidates. Without a route, it gives instructions to select START and FINAL instead of inventing route results.

21. API AND DATA FLOW DETAILS
-----------------------------

The Express API is assembled in `src/apiApp.ts` and uses local datasets and engines. The development server in `server.ts` mounts the API and Vite middleware together. In production, the built client is served from `dist` while the same local API remains available.

Typical flows:

Feature search:

    Browser search input
        -> GET /api/features
        -> local Mars catalog filter
        -> feature result list and map fly-to

Elevation lookup:

    Selected coordinate
        -> GET /api/elevation
        -> local MOLA-style elevation model
        -> elevation and terrain card

Route planning:

    START + FINAL + options
        -> POST /api/routes/plan
        -> local route engine and hazard analysis
        -> candidate routes and safety summary

Offline assistant:

    User question + current context
        -> POST /api/ask-marsway
        -> local intent matching and MarsWay data
        -> answer with source marker `marsway-offline-local`

The API is designed so the browser can remain interactive even when optional external tiles or image services are slow or unavailable.

22. DATA LIFECYCLE AND PROVENANCE
---------------------------------

Data enters the application through bundled TypeScript datasets, local catalog files, or public source references documented in the interface. Parsers normalize feature identifiers, names, coordinates, and feature classes. Engines then derive route, elevation, atmosphere, and science values from those normalized inputs.

Every important output should be interpreted through its provenance:

- Source-backed records are not the same as model outputs.
- A derived coordinate or distance is reproducible from source geometry.
- A modeled soil or hazard score is a planning proxy.
- A simulated dust or visual effect is for exploration, not measurement.

This separation is a core part of MarsWay's educational value.

25. MAINTENANCE GUIDELINES
--------------------------

- Keep route and safety calculations deterministic for the same inputs.
- Label new environmental values with their provenance.
- Add or update tests when changing route scoring or environmental formulas.
- Do not describe a modeled value as live unless a live source and timestamp are present.
- Keep assistant responses bounded to local data and supported intents.
- Avoid adding an external AI dependency for basic application help.
- Preserve mobile and desktop interaction paths when changing map overlays.
- Re-run lint, tests, build, and browser smoke checks before release.

26. LIMITATION INTERPRETATION
-----------------------------

The map is a visualization and educational planning surface. It is not a certified digital elevation model for every operational decision. The route engine cannot see every boulder, rut, dune edge, subsurface condition, wheel-soil interaction, radiation event, dust front, or communication constraint. Public imagery may be incomplete or unavailable. The offline assistant may not understand questions outside its supported language patterns and local knowledge.

These limitations are not hidden edge cases; they define how MarsWay should be used responsibly. The correct interpretation of a route result is: "This is the best result supported by the current local model and datasets," not: "This route is guaranteed safe."
